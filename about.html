History of Labyrinths

The labyrinth is one of the oldest and most enduring symbols known to humanity, with ancient forms found across southern Europe and North Africa from around 2000 BCE. Across many cultures, labyrinths have carried symbolic, ceremonial and spiritual meaning. Unlike a maze, a labyrinth is not a puzzle to be solved, but a path to be experienced. It follows a single winding route to the centre and back again, with no dead ends. The journey inward and outward can be seen as a metaphor for life itself. There is no one right way to walk a labyrinth. Many people use it as a walking meditation to slow down, reflect and reconnect. Its path can help ground and balance the walker, while offering perspective on whatever may be unfolding in life. Learn more!

The Merri Creek Labyrinth

The Merri Creek Labyrinth was created by the community, for the community. Created by local residents over the summer of 2001–2002, it has become a valued local place for wellbeing, reflection and connection. In 2026, the labyrinth and surrounding landscape were renewed through community effort and support, including new native plantings that strengthen habitat and beauty along Merri Creek. The labyrinth offers space to reconnect with yourself, with others and with nature. In an urban yet natural setting, it invites quiet walking, contemplation and pause. Event Enquiries

The labyrinth is located on public land and not a bookable venue. If you are planning to hold an event at the labyrinth, you may need to apply for an Event in Public Spaces permit. Details about permit requirements and how to submit an enquiry can be found on the City of Yarra website: https://www.yarracity.vic.gov.au/things-to-do/arts/plan-outdoor-event/events-public-spaces-permit
